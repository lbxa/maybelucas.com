I went from almost being expelled and failing maths in high school to the top student of my maths class, co-founder of the [first maths club](https://xyzmaths.org/) and an elected leader looking after 300 students. At 16, I wrote my [first neural net](https://mlp.lbxa.net/), [sold software to clients](https://lattice.lbxa.net/), built a nuclear computer, gave talks to audiences of hundreds and mentored university-level courses.

I created purpose by creating useful things.

I wanted to study at MIT, but my family couldn’t afford it. After ranking in the top 3%, I enrolled at the University of New South Wales in Sydney to study computer science and engineering.

During COVID, whilst all my friends were playing video games, I built software for several companies and got hired as one of the youngest senior consultants at PwC. I built an [ML platform](https://www.pwc.com.au/alliances/microsoft/the-evolution-of-auditing.html) serving 10k MAUs, served as tech lead for a [payroll compliance platform](https://www.pwc.com.au/workforce/payroll-analytics-lab.html) that saved large enterprises millions of dollars and led the firm’s first [applied research project](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09972), resulting in two [first-author publications](https://www.emerald.com/imds/article/125/11/3024/1253142/Toward-trustworthy-content-the-role-of-challengers). All before I’d even graduated.

During that time I also worked on fusion research, simulating promising materials for tokamak reactors on a supercomputer. I enjoyed tackling the biggest problems, so I quit my job, wrote an [100-page undergrad thesis](https://vla.lbxa.net/) on post-training a robot I built, loved it, dropped everything and applied to study maths at NYU. After graduating I spent a year building companies in consumer ([onexhq.com](http://onexhq.com/), [capsol.ai](http://capsol.ai/), [wherearepeople.com](http://wherearepeople.com/)) and enterprise ([shopstack.ai](http://shopstack.ai/), [axco.ai](http://axco.ai/)) working with great people like [Fred Schebesta](https://www.linkedin.com/in/fredschebesta/) before moving to NYC.

All my work has centred on how humans interact with machines, culminating in the ultimate interface: a general-purpose robot. I left an amazing life in Sydney to realize this mission \- at a scale only possible with America’s deep pools of risk capital.

In the words of Lao Tzu, “a journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step.” That next step is blue-sky research on the biggest bottleneck in robotics, scalable data collection.
